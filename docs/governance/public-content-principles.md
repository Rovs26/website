# KitaMo Public Content Principles

Status: **WEB-1 governance foundation; not final page copy**

Drafted: 2026-07-25
Effective status: **Pending approval**

These principles govern later public website content. They do not authorize
claims, legal text, launch messaging, navigation, or final marketing copy.

## Editorial priorities

### Clarity before persuasion

Help a reader understand what KitaMo is, who a statement applies to, and what
state it is in before attempting to persuade. Prefer concrete, bounded language
over slogans, superlatives, or buzzwords.

### Evidence before claims

Every factual product statement must have a current entry in
`docs/governance/public-claims-register.md`. Evidence and explicit publication
approval are separate requirements. Never invent a capability, statistic,
testimonial, partner, customer count, rating, certification, award, security
badge, price, or result.

### Current state before future vision

Lead with the current approved state. Testing, planned, and future capabilities
must never make the current product appear more available or complete than it
is. Do not turn a roadmap, prototype, draft, or possibility into a promise.

### Plain language

Use familiar words, short sentences, and direct explanations. Define necessary
terms without exposing internal architecture. Avoid jargon, unexplained
abbreviations, inflated technology language, and copy that requires technical
knowledge to interpret.

### Filipino-first relevance

Write for the practical context of Filipino micro-businesses. Use Filipino,
English, or Taglish intentionally, with terminology reviewed for audience
comprehension and regional fit. Familiarity must come from accurate context,
not stereotypes, forced slang, or assumptions about income, education, or
digital literacy.

### Explain AI practically

Mention AI only after the exact behavior, inputs, outputs, limitations, data
handling, human control, availability, and product-status category are
validated. Explain what a person can do and what remains their responsibility.
Do not use “AI magic,” anthropomorphism, autonomy claims, or vague intelligence
language.

## Trust and persuasion boundaries

Public content must follow all of these rules:

- **No fear-based selling.** Do not exploit anxiety about business failure,
  poverty, competitors, lost income, compliance, or technology.
- **No exaggerated income claims.** Do not suggest dramatic revenue, savings,
  growth, or profit without approved, representative evidence and context.
- **No guaranteed business results.** Never guarantee earnings, accuracy,
  growth, efficiency, cost reduction, or success.
- **No implied financial advice.** Product records or reports must not be framed
  as accounting, investment, lending, tax, or financial advice.
- **No fake urgency.** Do not invent deadlines, limited supply, expiring access,
  waitlists, countdowns, discounts, or launch pressure.
- **No dark patterns.** Do not hide choices, preselect consent, obstruct
  cancellation, manipulate comparison, shame a reader, or use misleading
  controls.
- **No hidden future-product disclaimers.** A status limitation must be adjacent
  to the relevant statement, readable, and as prominent as the future-facing
  idea. It cannot be buried in a footnote.
- **No legal text written as marketing.** Privacy, terms, consent, data-use, and
  rights language must be accurate, reviewable, and visually distinct from
  promotional copy.

Trust language is itself a claim. Words such as “secure,” “private,” “safe,”
“compliant,” “official,” “guaranteed,” and “certified” require specific,
current evidence and approval.

## Accessible, mobile-first content

### Accessible language

- Put the main point first.
- Use descriptive headings and link text that makes sense out of context.
- Explain unavoidable technical, legal, financial, or English-only terms.
- Avoid idioms when they reduce comprehension or translation quality.
- Use inclusive language and do not assume vision, hearing, motor ability,
  literacy level, device quality, or connection quality.
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images and captions/transcripts for
  meaningful media.
- Do not communicate meaning through color, icon, position, or tone alone.

### Concise mobile-first content

- Keep one primary idea per section.
- Front-load the decision-relevant fact and status.
- Prefer short paragraphs and scannable lists where they aid comprehension.
- Remove repetition before reducing necessary limitations or evidence.
- Keep labels explicit; do not rely on hover, fine print, or wide-screen layout
  to reveal critical context.

Concise does not mean incomplete. Availability, limitations, exclusions,
pricing, data-use, and legal context must remain visible when material.

## Product-status language

Use these labels consistently and exactly unless a later approved terminology
decision replaces them:

| Label             | Meaning                                                                                              | Content rule                                                         |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Available Today   | Implemented, verified, usable in the current approved release, and approved for public communication | State only the bounded approved capability and relevant limitations. |
| Currently Testing | Limited to internal testing, invited testers, a pilot, or another controlled environment             | Name the limited status plainly; do not imply general access.        |
| Planned           | Accepted into the roadmap but not currently available                                                | Do not state or imply a release date unless separately approved.     |
| Future Vision     | A directional possibility that may change                                                            | Make non-commitment explicit and avoid delivery language.            |

Each status treatment must include visible text and accessible semantics. Status
must never depend on color alone. A page must not visually emphasize a future
capability while making its status materially less prominent.

## Claim dating and revalidation

Date every sensitive product statement in its governance record. Sensitive
topics include:

- platform and release availability;
- testing track or pilot access;
- features and limitations;
- AI, analytics, synchronization, cloud, backup, accounts, and subscriptions;
- pricing, trials, discounts, and payment behavior;
- data handling, privacy, security, compliance, and accessibility;
- public domain, legal identity, and support contact.

For a public page, show an “updated” or “as of” date when the date materially
helps a reader interpret the statement. A visible date does not replace
revalidation.

Revalidate every claim before a major app or website release and whenever its
source, product state, wording, audience, language, or publication surface
changes. Outdated content must be removed or corrected; it must not be preserved
behind a quieter disclaimer.

## Content review gate

Before later public content is approved:

1. Identify the reader, decision, and page purpose.
2. Check every product and trust statement against the claims register.
3. Check governed words against
   `docs/governance/public-terminology.md`.
4. Assign the correct product-status label and present it accessibly.
5. Confirm current-state information precedes planned or future material.
6. Confirm material limitations and exclusions are adjacent and equally clear.
7. Review Filipino relevance, plain language, accessibility, and mobile
   scanning.
8. Remove unsupported persuasion, urgency, results, privacy, security, and
   compliance language.
9. Record the approver, approval date, source revision, scope, and revalidation
   trigger.

Failure at any gate keeps the content in draft. WEB-1 establishes this process
only; final public page copy belongs to later approved milestones.
